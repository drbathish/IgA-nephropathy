Study summary (for evidence-reviewer, not a substitute for full extraction): Three-phase pilot study. (1) Clinical: 70 children with IgAN, 43 with other glomerulopathies, 30 healthy controls; urinary exosomal C3a normalized to creatinine (uE-C3a/Cr) was elevated in IgAN and correlated with log 24h-urine-protein (ρ=0.480, p<0.001) and Oxford C (crescent) score (ρ=0.565, p<0.001); AUC for distinguishing IgAN from non-IgAN glomerulopathies was only 0.637 (modest, explicitly NOT presented as disease-specific) — uE-C3a/Cr decreased with remission and was higher in non-remission vs. remission patients on immunosuppressive therapy at 12 weeks (p<0.001). (2) In vivo: IgAN model rats showed upregulated renal exosomal C3a (rE-C3a), increased p-p65/t-p65 NF-κB ratio, and elevated MCP-1/IL-6. (3) In vitro: IgAN patient urinary exosomes promoted rat mesangial cell proliferation and NF-κB activation/cytokine release, partially reversed by a C3aR antagonist. Authors' own conclusion: uE-C3a/Cr is not a disease-specific diagnostic marker but may serve as a non-invasive disease-activity/treatment-response monitoring marker; C3aR flagged as a candidate therapeutic target. Explicitly described as a small pilot study requiring larger-cohort validation — flag for evidence-reviewer's quality appraisal (small n, modest AUC, exploratory).
